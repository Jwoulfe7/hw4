# The Campus Customs Look

*Being a Short Guide to the House Style, with Notes on Why It Sells*

Look, the shop was perfectly nice. Nice is for motels. A Yale store should feel like the inside of your grandfather's closet in Edgartown: navy, rope, brass, felt, and a little smug about it. We made every element earn its place, and every place sells something.

---

## The Fundamentals

**The cloth.** The whole site sits on oxford cloth, a faint basket weave under every page.
*Why:* nobody consciously notices a texture, but everyone notices when there isn't one. It feels like a garment, not a template.

**The type.** Three typefaces, three jobs:
- **Oswald** for headlines (it shouts, politely),
- **Libre Caslon** for the voice (it went to boarding school),
- **Pinyon Script** for monograms and sign-offs (it summers).

*Why:* the store should *sound* the same everywhere, so it does.

**The colors.** Navy, Nantucket red, sailcloth, brass. Madras is permitted in small doses, like gin.

---

## The Particulars

**I. The Crest.** A navy shield, rope-stitched, with a script *Y* and a "Bulldog Blue" ribbon. It's in the nav, on the membership card and in the footer.
*Why:* a crest says *institution*. Institutions get trusted with credit cards.

**II. The Ticker.** House notes roll across the top: "Navy is a neutral," "Returns within 30 days, tags on," "Press / to summon the Valet."
*Why:* it slips the return policy and the shortcuts in as charm, not small print. Shoppers who know they can return things buy more of them.

**III. The Rope.** A length of rope pays out across the top of the page as you scroll.
*Why:* progress you can see keeps people scrolling to the end. Rope is simply the correct material for it.

**IV. The Hang Tags.** Every product card hangs from a brass eyelet on red-and-white string, with the price on a notched tag. Hover and it swings.
*Why:* it looks like a real shop floor, and touching is the first step to buying. The swing makes browsing feel like flipping through a rack.

**V. The Pennants.** The collections (Colleges, Varsity, Family, Grad Schools) are felt pennants that wave when you hover.
*Why:* nobody ever hung a dropdown menu in a dorm room. Pennants make "shop by affiliation" feel like belonging, and belonging is the whole product.

**VI. The Uniforms.** Four outfits for real occasions: *The Game*, *Parents' Weekend*, *The Regatta*, *Reading Period*. Each shows a flat lay, the pieces, and the full price from live catalogue prices. "Lay it out" puts the outfit on the Bulldog's Picks shelf.
*Why:* nobody needs a crewneck. Everybody needs an outfit for The Game. Selling the occasion turns one item into three, and that's what raises the basket size.

**VII. The Handbook Page.** The house rules are typeset as page 64 of a book, with a running head, a chapter title, Roman numerals and a gentle tilt.
*Why:* humor builds loyalty. People screenshot it, send it to their roommate, and come back.

**VIII. The Belts.** Sections are divided by grosgrain ribbon belts with brass D-rings. The footer wears madras.
*Why:* even a section divider should be something you could buy, and should want to.

**IX. The Monogrammer.** Type your initials on any product and see them on a sewn-on felt patch in gold, white or Nantucket red. It's clearly marked as a preview.
*Why:* once it has your initials on it, it's already yours. That's the oldest trick in retail, done with taste and honesty.

**X. "How to Wear It, per the Handbook."** Every product page has an index card, pinned with a red tack, giving one firm opinion. Crewnecks: "Over a button-down, collar out."
*Why:* advice turns a product page into a styling session. Confident shoppers don't abandon their carts.

**XI. The Stamp.** An "Officially Licensed" rubber stamp, slightly crooked, on every product photo.
*Why:* licensing is the shop's best argument against cheap knockoffs. Now you can't miss it.

**XII. The Membership Card.** Log in and Create Account are a navy club card, "The Bulldog Blue Club", with a crest and member number. Once you're in, your initials appear in a monogram roundel in the nav.
*Why:* nobody wants to "create an account". Everybody wants to join a club. Members come back.

**XIII. The Life Ring.** The chat button is a red-and-white life preserver that spins when you hover. The Bulldog signs with a script *B* and writes on cream stationery.
*Why:* a life ring means *help is right here*, which is what a chat button should say. Help that's easy to find means fewer shoppers leaving confused.

**XIV. The Signal Flags.** The footer flies Yankee, Alfa, Lima, Echo on a halyard: *YALE*, in the International Code of Signals. Signed *"Yours in navy, the Bulldog."*
*Why:* it's an inside joke that rewards scrolling to the bottom, and a store with inside jokes is a store with regulars.

**XV. Lost at Sea.** A missing page gets a compass rose that searches for north, a nudge toward the Harvard Coop, and "Return to port."
*Why:* a dead end with a joke and two exits keeps shoppers in the store.

---

## The Rules We Kept

- **Nothing is invented.** No fake sales, no made-up founding year, no promised monogramming. The ticker states true facts, and the Uniforms charge the catalogue price with no "bundle discount".
- **The essentials stay plain.** Prices, stock and buttons stay high-contrast and readable. The theatre is around them, never on top of them.
- **Restraint where it counts.** Shoppers who've turned on "reduce motion" on their device get no swinging, waving or spinning. Phones get every page with no sideways scrolling.
